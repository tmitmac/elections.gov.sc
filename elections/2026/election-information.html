Understanding the system

How does MMP work?

Mixed-Member Proportional representation combines local electorate representation with party-list representation. It is designed to make a party's overall share of parliamentary seats broadly reflect its share of the party vote, subject to the applicable electoral rules.

Explore the voting system

1. Electorate vote

Vote for the candidate you want to represent your local electorate in Parliament. The candidate with the most votes wins the electorate, under the stated election rules.

2. Party vote

Vote for the political party you want represented in Parliament. This vote is used to calculate each party's overall entitlement to parliamentary seats.

Electorate representation

Members elected in individual electorates represent communities throughout Square Country.

Party-list representation

List seats help adjust parliamentary representation to reflect the party vote, in accordance with the electoral system's seat-allocation rules.

MMP stands for Mixed-Member Proportional. Voters cast an electorate vote for a local candidate and a party vote. The party vote determines the overall distribution of parliamentary seats, subject to the applicable electoral rules and eligibility requirements.
Your electorate vote supports a candidate in your local electorate. Your party vote supports a political party and is used to calculate its overall representation in Parliament. You can choose different parties and candidates for your two votes.
