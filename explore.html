How does MMP work?

MMP combines local representation with proportional representation. Here is how your vote moves from the ballot paper to Parliament.

Understanding your ballot

Two votes, two different purposes

Both votes matter, but they affect the election in different ways.

Your electorate vote

This vote determines who represents your local electorate in Parliament. You choose one candidate standing in your electorate.

  • Vote for one candidate.
  • The candidate with the most votes wins the seat.
  • You can choose a candidate from any party, where they are standing.

Your party vote

This vote indicates which political party you want represented in Parliament. It determines each eligible party's proportional share of seats, subject to the electoral rules.

  • Vote for one political party.
  • Your party vote helps determine the overall balance of Parliament.
  • You may vote for a different party from your electorate candidate.
From votes to seats

How are parliamentary seats allocated?

Parliament combines electorate MPs with party-list MPs. The party vote establishes each eligible party's overall entitlement, while electorate results determine who wins local seats.

Electorate seats

Square Country has 13 electorates. Each electorate elects one representative, giving voters a local MP to represent their area.

  • 13 local electorates
  • One winning candidate per electorate
  • Local representation in Parliament

Party-list seats

Party-list MPs help bring a party's total number of MPs into line with its party-vote entitlement. A party's electorate MPs count towards that entitlement.

  • Seats are allocated using the party vote
  • Electorate MPs count towards a party's total
  • List MPs help address the proportional balance
4% Party-vote threshold

When can a party qualify for list seats?

Under the Electoral Act 1986, a party generally needs at least 4% of the party vote to qualify for proportional representation through the party-list allocation. An electorate-seat win may provide an alternative route to parliamentary representation. Check the official election rules for the precise eligibility and allocation provisions.
